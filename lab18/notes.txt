Lab 18 - notes (Part 2)

What exactly does thread_switch save and load?

It saves and loads only the callee-saved registers of a thread: ra, sp and s0..s11
(14 registers of 8 bytes, 112 bytes in total). First it stores the registers of the
thread that is running into old (a0), at the offsets of struct context (ra at 0, sp at 8,
s0 at 16, ... s11 at 104). Then it loads the same 14 registers of the next thread from
new (a1). The other registers (a0-a7, t0-t6) are caller-saved: by the RISC-V calling
convention the caller of thread_switch does not expect them to survive a function call,
so the compiler has already saved what it needs on the thread's own stack. The program
counter is not saved directly: ra plays its role. Because sp is swapped too, every thread
continues on its own stack, with its own local variables.

Why is ra the register that makes execution continue in the other thread?

thread_switch ends with ret, and ret jumps to the address in ra. Before ret, ra was
loaded from the next thread's context, so ret does not go back to the caller of the old
thread but to the place where the next thread itself called thread_switch the last time
(inside its thread_schedule), and that thread continues from there. For a new thread,
thread_create put the address of the thread function in ctx.ra and the top of its stack
in ctx.sp, so the first switch "returns" into the start of thread_a, thread_b or thread_c,
on the thread's own stack.
